Data Sources

This visualization compiles data from multiple verified sources:

  • K-12 School Shooting Database - Every US K-12 school shooting since 1970
  • The Violence Project - Mass Shooter Database with demographic data
  • Gun Violence Archive - Near real-time US gun violence data
  • Wikipedia - Comprehensive lists of school shootings globally
  • World Population Review - Country comparison data

Definitions

School shooting: An incident where a firearm was discharged at a school, university, or educational institution, resulting in casualties.

Perpetrator demographics: Race/ethnicity classifications follow US Census categories. Where perpetrators have mixed heritage, they are classified as "Mixed" unless self-identified otherwise.

Limitations

  • This visualization shows major incidents with detailed data. The K-12 SSDB documents 2,400+ incidents in the US alone; most are lower-casualty events not individually mapped here.
  • International data is less complete than US data due to reporting differences.
  • Religion data is available for fewer than 10% of perpetrators and is excluded from demographic analysis.
  • Some historical coordinates are approximate.
  • Terrorist attacks at schools (Beslan, Peshawar, Garissa) are included but noted separately as they differ in motive and scale from individual school shootings.

Per-Capita Methodology

Per-capita comparisons use 2020 US Census population data. Rates are calculated as (number of perpetrators of group / population of group) normalized per million. These rates reflect the dataset's incidents, not all gun violence.

Comparison Mode

Comparison mode allows side-by-side analysis of two demographic groups. Red markers represent Group A, blue markers represent Group B. The "X times more likely" calculation compares per-capita rates using relevant population data (US Census for domestic comparisons, World Bank for international).

Note on Completeness

No dataset of school shootings is fully complete. This visualization prioritizes accuracy over comprehensiveness. If you notice an error or missing incident, the data is open for correction.
